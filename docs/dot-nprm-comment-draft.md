**Re: Proposed amendment to 14 CFR 399.84(a) (full-fare advertising)**

I am an independent researcher building a consumer price measure — a
replication-cost index that tracks what it costs households to reproduce a
prior period's travel experience, published as a spread over official CPI
series. I write to comment on one narrow, practical consequence of the
proposed rule: the availability of the posted, all-in airfare as public
measurement infrastructure.

The current rule's requirement that the total price be the most prominently
stated figure does more than protect consumers at the point of sale. It
creates a consistent, publicly observable price record that independent
measurement — academic, journalistic, and statistical — depends on. The
Bureau of Labor Statistics' own airline-fares methodology collects posted
fares inclusive of mandatory taxes and fees; researchers replicating,
auditing, or extending such measurement rely on the same posted totals. When
component prices may be displayed as prominently as the total, the effective
price of a ticket becomes harder to observe consistently across carriers and
across time, and the public record of air-travel prices degrades accordingly.

I take no position here on the advertising-format questions at the center of
the proposal. I ask only that the Department weigh, as a distinct cost, the
degradation of the public price record: whatever flexibility is granted in
display prominence, the rule should preserve a requirement that the full
total price remain published, machine-consistently locatable, and no less
accessible than any component price. A minimal safeguard — retaining
399.84(a)'s requirement that the total price be stated and easily located,
even if prominence rules are relaxed — would preserve the measurement value
of the posted fare at negligible cost to advertisers.
