## Seat I — index-number theory

*Seat priors:* Sympathetic to measurement innovation, ruthless on formal claims: Konüs theory, index-number formulae, characteristics space, partial identification.

**Simulated verdict:** Major revision: the sector-level instrument is acceptable in principle once its theorems are restated and its primitive fixed; the RX-100 corridor as constructed is not yet an acceptable full-basket estimand.

### Simulated summary assessment

Let me begin with what this seat is disposed to credit, because it is real. The conditional replication-cost primitive is correctly named and genuinely unoccupied: a Leontief-in-characteristics conditional cost function minimized over a posted menu, published as a spread, is a coherent measurement object with an honest ancestry (R-CPI-SC, the 2009 PPI fee adjustment), and the event grammar's refusal to print on menu expansion (P2) plus mandatory negative events (P3) shows more index-theoretic discipline than most agency satellite series. The pre-commitment apparatus — bands before inputs, executable worked examples, logged downward corrections — is the strongest governance I have seen from a non-agency team, and I say so before I take the design apart.

The formal core, however, is under-proved relative to the confidence of its prose, and in one place it is wrong. P4's "bound theorem" proves a level inequality and asserts a ratio inequality; the missing denominator condition is base-period optimality of the frozen experience for the preferences being bounded — a revealed-preference assumption the project elsewhere claims to have expunged — and a two-line counterexample built from the project's own phantom-loss population defeats the claim as stated. Beneath that sits a worse problem: the primitive z is defined as the purchased configuration in §1–§2 and computed as the utilized experience in §4 and Phase 1, and the flagship S2/S3 prints change sign and magnitude depending on which reading one takes. Dominance is evaluated on a projection onto a finite frozen dictionary, which silently restricts the preference class for which any bound holds; the interval devices ([0, gap], [0%,100%]) have no stated propagation algebra through the Young mean, the sum-to-one share constraint, or the annual chain; and the Gap confounds the pricing concept with an arithmetic-vs-geometric formula wedge that, on the spec's own S3 numbers, is several times larger than the +0.10pp predicted residual the honesty-exhibit machinery treats as a meaningful prediction. Every one of these is fixable, and none is fixed.

The corridor is where my sympathy ends. G* is presented as a partially identified parameter, but on the strata that dominate its width the parameter is not unidentified — it is undefined: the spec's own non-emptiness rule (§3.2) exits non-reproducible experiences from the measure, and the tier map concedes OER (24% of the true-B40 basket) has "no transaction menu at all." Tier B's interval either collapses to the zero-imputation the proposal was written to kill (empty registers at first print) or defaults to A-K and yields a first print roughly 4.6pp wide before Tier R's own intervals — sign unidentifiable for years, given the audit program's own certification ceiling. And the Tier-A narrowing mechanism runs on a concept switch A-T does not cover: per-unit audits certify that CPI tracks posted prices within matched identity, a proposition nobody disputes, and the corridor converts that certificate into a bound on the disputed replication object. My answer to §7 Q5: I reject the corridor as drafted; the construction I would accept is a defined-domain corridor over the reproducible partition — strata where ρ_i has semantics — with the uncovered share published as uncovered, riding beside the point-identified reweighting exhibit. That is a smaller claim than "an interval over 100% of the basket." It is also the only one of the two I could sign.

### Major objections and prepared responses

#### F-1 · P4 proves a level inequality but claims a ratio inequality: the Konüs denominator condition is missing, and the repair contradicts the project's own posture

**Target:** Spec v00.02.01 §2.2 (elementary link relative), §2.4 P4, §11 (revealed-preference row); memo ¶1 (Konüs upper-bound language 'in every artifact')

**Simulated objection.** P4 states: if the base bundle was cost-minimal for z^{t-1} on M_{t-1}, then for any V with V(z^{t-1}) = u, C_V(u;t) ≤ C(z^{t-1};t), 'hence ρ_t ≥ the conditional Konüs relative.' The 'hence' is a non sequitur. The Konüs relative is C_V(u;t)/C_V(u;t-1); the numerator inequality is fine (granting monotonicity of V, which is nowhere stated), but the denominators run the wrong way: C_V(u;t-1) ≤ C(z^{t-1};t-1) always, because the set of bundles achieving utility u contains the set dominating z. Cost-minimality *for the vector z* — the stated hypothesis — is not cost-minimality *for the utility level u*, which is what the Laspeyres–Konüs argument actually requires as denominator equality. Counterexample using the project's own phantom-loss population (§9.1): base menu Basic $260 / Economy $300; a household indifferent to the carry-on buys Economy anyway; z (purchased configuration) includes the carry-on, so C(z;t-1) = 300 while C_V(u;t-1) = 260, and every P4 hypothesis holds. Event year, pure scalar repricing: Basic $560, Economy $600. Then ρ = 2.000 but the conditional Konüs relative is 560/260 ≈ 2.154 — RX *understates* it and the upper-bound claim, shipped unconditionally 'in every release,' is false. The only repair is to assume z^{t-1} was cost-minimizing for u at t-1 — i.e., base-period revealed-preference optimality of the purchase, across sellers for the same-seller headline — which is exactly the welfare assumption §11 boasts was 'dropped project-wide.' Note also that even repaired, the bound aggregates only profile-by-profile: the Young mean of per-profile bounds bounds a share-weighted average of heterogeneous conditional Konüs relatives, a Prais-style plutocratic object whose identity the spec never states.

**What would satisfy the referee.** Restate P4 with its full hypothesis set — V weakly monotone in ledger attributes; z^{t-1} cost-minimizing at t-1 for u over the choice set the COLI concept permits (same-seller for H, all sellers for S) — and publish it as a named assumption beside Q, not as a theorem; or retreat to the defensible level inequality plus a conditional statement of the ratio bound, propagated into every artifact where unconditional upper-bound language now appears. An appendix proof acknowledging the counterexample class, and a one-sentence statement of what the aggregate bound bounds.

**Team response — ARGUABLE.** Conceded on the logic: as written, P4's 'hence' does not follow — cost-minimality for the vector z is not cost-minimality for the utility level u, and your $260/$300 household defeats the ratio claim for its preferences. But the repair is already half-built into the operational design rather than contradicting it, and we will restate P4 in two parts, both true. Part (a), unconditional: with the denominator computed as C(z; t−1) on the archived t−1 menu — the convention the Phase 1 in-sample regime already pins (prereg §11 regime (ii) anchors each profile's t−1 denominator 'exactly as codebook §1 defines C(z; t−1)', i.e., the archived-menu min, not the raw transaction price) — ρ_t is the Konüs relative of the conditional preference anchored at z, by identity: the anchored Leontief-in-characteristics cost function over the menu-purchase choice set IS the replication program. That is the constant-experience object the release language names, so the headline bound sentence survives with no revealed-preference content at all. Part (b), conditional: for any weakly monotone V with V(z)=u, the level inequality C_V(u;t) ≤ C(z;t) is unconditional; the ratio bound ρ_t ≥ C_V(u;t)/C_V(u;t−1) additionally requires C_V(u;t−1) = C(z;t−1) — base-period cost-minimality of the frozen experience for u — which we will publish as a named assumption beside Q, not as a theorem, with your counterexample class worked in an appendix. Note that under the operational primitive (z = utilized experience; see F-2) your household sits in the personal-item profile: its z excludes the carry-on, its denominator is $260, and its print is 2.154 — exactly its conditional Konüs relative, not an understatement. The counterexample is generated jointly by the entitlement reading and the transaction-price denominator, both of which we are amending. On aggregation: agreed and adopted — §2.2 gains one sentence stating that the Young mean of per-profile relatives tracks the growth of the base population's aggregate replication cost, i.e., a base-expenditure-share-weighted average of heterogeneous per-profile conditional relatives (a plutocratic object within the B40 target population), and the per-profile bound aggregates only in that share-weighted sense.

#### F-2 · The frozen vector z is defined as purchased entitlement in §1–§2 and computed as utilized experience in §4 and Phase 1 — the flagship exhibit's sign depends on which, and each reading breaks something

**Target:** Spec §1 ('every attribute included in the base-period purchase'), §2.1 (z = 'configuration actually purchased'), §4.1 S1–S3; codebook §1; prereg §3 (AIR-P2 'personal item only')

**Simulated objection.** Under §2.1's definition, the S2 base menu contains only Economy $300, so the personal-item profile's *purchased* configuration includes the carry-on entitlement; Basic $260 does not weakly dominate it, and P0 prints 0% in S2 — not the −13.3% the spec prints and calls the design's proudest moment. Worse, in S1 the entitlement reading makes P0 print +28.3% (its cheapest dominating bundle is Economy $385), collapsing the S3 showcase from +19.9% to a uniform +28.3% and erasing the incidence-weighting exhibit entirely. The Phase 1 profiles quietly adopt the opposite, utilization reading ('RT nonstop; personal item only'), which contradicts §1's 'includes every discretely contractible attribute that was included in the base-period purchase' and the memo's 'including formerly-included features.' The fork is first-order: entitlement-freezing systematically raises ρ (more binding constraints); utilization-freezing lowers it. And each reading breaks a different formal claim: entitlement-freezing generates the F-1 counterexample directly, while utilization-freezing breaks the denominator convention — §2.2 says the denominator 'normally equals the observed t−1 transaction price,' but a utilization-frozen z has C(z;t−1) strictly below the transaction price whenever the purchase was a superset of use ($260 vs $300 in my example), so the observed-price denominator again destroys the ratio bound unless C(z;t−1) is recomputed as a min on the archived t−1 menu. As it stands the elementary object of the index is not well-defined, and the two candidate definitions produce different signs on the exhibit the whole packet leads with.

**What would satisfy the referee.** One primitive, chosen and propagated: presumably z = the profile's utilized experience vector, with entitlements entering only through the profile partition and incidence weights; §1, §2.1, trigger T1, and the Ledger schema rewritten to match; the denominator convention restated as C(z;t−1) computed on the archived t−1 menu, with the observed transaction price used only when they coincide; S1–S3 re-derived under the chosen primitive; and an explicit statement of what 'formerly-included features' means once utilization is the primitive.

**Team response — ARGUABLE.** The primitive is the utilized experience vector — and the referee has caught the spec's definitional sentences contradicting the spec's own computations, not an unresolved fork. Everything that computes already implements utilization: the engine fixture defines the personal-item profile as z = {trip} with base cost $300 (P0 in engine/test_restoration.py), which is precisely what makes S2 print 0% / −13.3% / ≈−4.0% and S3 print +19.9% — the numbers the spec ships and declares executable; Phase 1's AIR-P2 is 'personal item only'; §4.1's own sentence is 'the traveler who never carried a bag has an unchanged replication cost'; and §9.1's phantom-loss discipline is incoherent under the entitlement reading, since a non-user's entitlement would manufacture exactly the loss §9.1 exists to kill. So §1's 'every attribute included in the base-period purchase' and §2.1's 'configuration actually purchased' are drafting errors relative to the design, and we amend them: z = the attribute vector of the experience actually consumed in the base period, with entitlements entering only through the profile partition, T1's documentary evidence, and incidence weights. Your denominator point is right and the fix is the one you name, which prereg §11 regime (ii) already pins for every in-sample link: §2.2's 'normally equals the observed t−1 transaction price' becomes 'equals C(z; t−1) computed on the archived t−1 menu; the observed transaction price is the usual evidence for it and coincides whenever the base purchase was the min-cost dominating bundle.' 'Formerly-included features' then has a precise meaning: attributes delivered and experienced as inclusions of the base configuration — ad-free playback for every ad-free subscriber, the carry-on for those who carried one — that the current menu strips or prices separately. The re-derivation of S1–S3 under the chosen primitive is a no-op (they were computed under it, and the unit tests pin them), which we will state in the changelog with the tests cited as the invariant.

#### F-3 · Dominance is evaluated on a projection of characteristics space: P2 and P4 hold only for preferences measurable with respect to the frozen dictionary, and compound (non-nested) menu events have no decomposition rule

**Target:** Spec §2.4 P2/P4, §3.1 T3, §3.3; codebook §3 (streaming dictionary: resolution 'recorded, not priced'), §4

**Simulated objection.** The replication program's constraint Σ a_j q_j ⪰ z runs over the ledger's finite coordinate set, and weak dominance in that projection is not weak dominance in the space consumers occupy. The codebook itself prices ad-free playback but records resolution without pricing it: a cheaper 'ad-free, 720p' tier weakly dominates a frozen 4K ad-free experience in the projection and prints a genuine decrease under the P2 refinement while degrading a coordinate the project's own dictionary names. So 'menu expansion can never raise RX' is a true statement about the operator and a false statement about the economics whenever the expansion degrades on un-ledgered coordinates — and the negative prints P3 makes mandatory are only credible for preferences measurable with respect to the dictionary. That measurability restriction is also the real preference class of P4, and it excludes precisely the consumers whose un-priced degradation (skimpflation, §3.3) motivates the measure; the spec states the theorem without it and confines the concession to the Exclusion Register's small print. Separately, P2's hypothesis — 'adding a product without changing existing ones' — is an idealized comparative static that almost no actual event satisfies: Netflix added the ad tier while retiring Basic; Southwest repriced, renamed, and restructured simultaneously. Trigger T3 demands a cost rise 'attributable to the menu change, not to a pure scalar price move,' which presupposes a decomposition of compound menu changes into expansion, contraction, and repricing components that is defined nowhere; the prereg's fixed-denominator convention resolves it for named-fee events only. For everything else, T3's attribution is operator judgment wearing an axiom's clothes.

**What would satisfy the referee.** An explicit statement in §2.4 that P2/P4 are relative to the dictionary sigma-algebra, with the release-language consequence spelled out; a pre-registered decomposition rule for compound menu events (e.g., compute C on the full new menu and attribute via the route hierarchy, with a stated ordering convention), demonstrated on a worked non-nested example — the Netflix Basic retirement is sitting in the packet; and a statement of which negative prints are robust to un-ledgered degradation and which are not.

**Team response — ARGUABLE.** Three parts, two adopted and one contested in part. (i) Measurability: correct, and we will state it where it belongs — P2/P3/P4 are claims about the ledgered coordinate set; a negative print certifies a replication-cost decrease over the dictionary's coordinates, and preferences that turn on un-ledgered coordinates are outside the theorems. That sentence goes into §2.4 and the release boilerplate, not the Exclusion Register's small print. (ii) The 720p case is a codebook ambiguity we will resolve in the direction that protects the mandatory negative prints: every coordinate recorded in the attribute dictionary enters the dominance constraint; only priced coordinates generate line items. 'Recorded, not priced' then means resolution carries no attribute-level price series — not that a lower-resolution tier can dominate a frozen 4K experience. Under the disambiguated rule your ad-free 720p tier does not dominate, no false decrease prints, and the conceded boundary retreats to genuinely un-ledgered coordinates (skimpflation proper), where the spec already locates it. (iii) Compound events: here we push back on the framing while accepting the deliverable. The published ρ needs no decomposition — C is a min over the full posted new menu, so a simultaneous retirement, rename, and repricing is computed, not attributed; the route hierarchy, superset flag, and menu-removal grammar label the event (the Netflix Basic retirement is already in the ledger as exactly that: event_type menu-removal, tier-spread route), and same-seller scalar drift nets out of the Gap because it sits in both ρ and π. T3's attribution is load-bearing only where a decomposition actually enters arithmetic — the archival-regime event contributions — and there the convention is pre-registered with 'no deflator and no estimated decomposition' (fixed denominators for named-fee events; tier intervals re-baselined on the current menu's non-superset floor, pinned executable in test_interval_baselines_on_todays_menu_not_base_cost). What is fairly demanded and missing is the worked non-nested example in the spec itself; we will add the Netflix Basic retirement as an executable §4 example, and state which negative prints are robust to un-ledgered degradation (those on priced-and-recorded coordinates) and which are dictionary-relative.

#### F-4 · The Gap confounds pricing concept with aggregation formula — arithmetic Young over profiles against geometric CPI elementaries — and the 0.5pp chained-vs-direct threshold is arbitrary in level, horizon, and consequence

**Target:** Spec §2.2, §2.3, §2.5; codebook §9, §10; prereg §11 (predicted residual Gap +0.10pp)

**Simulated objection.** ρ_i is a base-share arithmetic (Young) mean over profiles; π_i is, for most CPI item strata under BLS's published Jevons criteria, a geometric mean over quotes. The spread ρ_i − π_i therefore carries a pure formula wedge — order half the cross-profile variance of relatives — on top of the pricing-concept difference: second-order in quiet years, first-order in exactly the event years the index exists for. On the spec's own S3 numbers the arithmetic mean of the three profile relatives is +19.8% while the geometric mean at the same shares is +19.1%: a 0.76pp wedge from formula alone, several times the +0.10pp predicted residual Gap on which the Southwest honesty exhibit stakes the project's credibility. The mandatory two-way decomposition (pricing-concept vs weighting) has no third column for this, so a reader cannot tell how much of any published Gap is menu economics and how much is Jensen's inequality. On chaining: the Young index fails time reversal, and an annually chained Young with re-frozen shares accumulates drift whose only control is codebook §10's 'divergence > 0.5pp cumulative triggers a methods note.' Why 0.5? Cumulative over what horizon? An unnormalized cumulative threshold triggers mechanically as the series lengthens and means nothing when it does; and the consequence — a note, not a re-benchmark or a dual publication — has no bite. The spec cites Szulc and Ivancic–Diewert–Fox to refuse monthly chaining, but adopts the annual chain with no drift theory at all, only a diagnostic with an unmotivated number attached.

**What would satisfy the referee.** A per-stratum formula-effect column in every release: RX recomputed under geometric aggregation over profiles (or the comparator recomposed arithmetically), so the pricing-concept effect is formula-clean; the drift threshold derived from something stated (e.g., the smallest divergence that could flip a published Gap's sign, or a per-link tolerance with a horizon), with a pre-committed consequence — publish both chained and direct, or re-benchmark — rather than a methods note.

**Team response — ARGUABLE.** Half conceded, half defended — and the defended half still yields the column you asked for. Defended: the arithmetic Young over profiles is not an aggregation slip; it is the pricing concept. Profiles partition people, not substitutable varieties: the share-weighted arithmetic mean of relatives is exactly the growth of the aggregate cost of restoring the base population's frozen experiences, while a geometric mean across profiles would embed unit-elastic substitution between experiences — the coping margin the measure exists to refuse. So part of ρ − π in an event year is genuinely 'Jevons on the CPI side versus fixed-experience arithmetic on ours,' and that is concept, not confound. Conceded: the reader currently cannot tell how much, and your S3 arithmetic (≈0.75pp of formula wedge at the spec's own shares) shows the wedge is first-order exactly in the event years the index exists for. The mandatory decomposition therefore gains a third column: RX recomputed under geometric aggregation at the same frozen shares, per stratum, every release, so the pricing-concept effect is formula-clean. One scope correction, offered without blunting your point: the +0.10pp Southwest figure is not differenced against a Jevons aggregate — it is an incidence-weighted event contribution over the matched-carrier scalar-fare comparator (prereg §11), a deliberately different object — but the criticism applies with full force to the in-sample regime, which is where the formula column will live. Also conceded in full: codebook §10's 0.5pp is a tripwire with no derivation, no horizon, and no bite. *Seat priors:* A career defending CPI methodology against motivated critiques: pre-commitment forensics, selection-on-the-outcome, fairness to CPI, inference, misuse.

**Simulated verdict:** Major revision: the validation exercise does not yet validate, the incidence primary that carries the G0 pass is underived, and the corridor is not yet an acceptable publication object; sign-off withheld pending the responses specified per objection.

### Simulated summary assessment

This is, by a wide margin, the most disciplined artifact I have been asked to review from the CPI-understates-it genre. The team has internalized the failure modes of its predecessors: the measure ships only as a spread, the upper-bound framing is genuinely everywhere, the trigger grammar is symmetric on paper, negative and zero prints are designed in, the shrinkflation claim was demoted before I could demolish it, and the corrections trail (the coverage restatement from ~30-35% down to 18.9-20.7%, the Seam-2 reconciliation) shows a posture I rarely see. Spec §5 quotes the airline-fares factsheet correctly and concedes the 80% first-bag designation. I say all this so the team understands that what follows is not a genre reflex.

It is nonetheless a report recommending major revision, for three structural reasons. First, the pre-commitment story — the packet's load-bearing credibility claim, the thing the cover memo tells my seat to attack first — does not survive contact with the repository's own history. The entire sequence from first spec to official G0 pass spans roughly twelve hours of author-controlled local commits; the spec containing every input the band would later 'predict' was committed two hours before the band; the conventions were 'pinned before computation' 150 seconds before a commit containing a passing dry run; and the one genuinely free parameter in the gate (base-period incidence, 45%) is underived from its cited source, whose coherent readings (58-78%) put the gate at marginal-to-failing. What G0 demonstrates is that the team's arithmetic is consistent with the literature the band was drawn from. That is not nothing, but it is not validation, and the memo's phrase 'before any inputs existed' is contradicted by the repo itself. Second, everything the project will actually publish first was selected on a known outcome — the positive exhibits known positive, the controls known negative or null before their gates were written — so the launch set has no capacity to surprise, and the sampling-frame discipline that would fix this is deferred to instruments (row 12, RX-100) that are not yet binding. Third, the corridor converts 'we measure ~2% of the basket' into 'we bound 100% of it' at the price of a width (~2K over three-quarters of the basket, certified share zero at first print by the team's own promotion arithmetic) that makes the object decision-irrelevant, anchored by a K whose mock value is already exceeded by the project's own only measured sector gap. The honest summary of the packet is: a genuinely novel pricing primitive, validated so far only against itself, whose flagship sector is 0.43% of the target population's basket and whose full-basket ambition currently adds rhetoric, not information. The revisions I specify are severable and mostly executable; several require only candor about what the machinery does and does not establish.

#### G-5 · Symmetry holds in the rule text but not in the detection process: the Improvements Register is three unverified candidates (two dead on arrival), the admissibility bar is deliberately stricter on the improvements side, and ordinary Tier-B strata with silent registers collapse to the exact zero-imputation the corridor claims to abolish

**Target:** docs/rx-100-audit-protocols.md §7; data/improvements-register.json; docs/rx-100-proposal.md §2 (Tier B), §3 (A-R, A-Q); codebook §4

**Simulated objection.** The negative-event rule is symmetric as written, and the banking control shows the engine can print a negative. But the corridor's Tier-B bounds turn symmetry from a rule property into an evidence-generation property, and there the design is asymmetric three times over. First, volume: the Exclusion side is fed by an active monthly collection apparatus in covered sectors and by an entire advocacy-adjacent literature (CEA, Senate PSI, FTC, CFPB) everywhere else; the Improvements Register at review time contains three candidate entries, none evidence-grade, of which two are explicitly 'CORRIDOR-DEAD' machinery demonstrations and the third is a non-event by rule. So M_i has an industrial supply chain and I_i has a scaffold. Second, admissibility: §7 candidly renames what 'was previously shipped as parity' — M_i admits diffuse third-party-monetized degradations while I_i additionally requires menu-adjacency and reverse-route monetizability — and correctly notes the bias direction is 'the opposite of comfortable': understating I_i invalidates L, quietly breaking the bracket. The proposed patches (rejection log, bounty) are not a detection apparatus; a bounty finds what motivated third parties look for, and the parties motivated to document quiet improvements in women's-apparel or used-car strata do not exist. Third, and worst: for an ordinary Tier-B stratum with unpopulated registers, the proposal's own arithmetic (band = [−I/E, +M/E] ∩ [−K, K]) yields [0,0] — a point at zero. #### [statistical-inference] The census-not-sample posture is a category error relative to the published claims

**Objection.** The Phase 1 pre-registration's preamble declares 'this is a census of posted menus, not a sample — no standard errors are claimed anywhere in Phase 1,' and the grocery section repeats it ('a census of a frozen basket, not a sample,' g1-band GB-ii). But a census claim is only true of the observed coordinates: 18 routes selected by T-100 passenger volume (a business- and hub-weighted ranking, not a B40 travel distribution), one itinerary shape (RT nonstop, first-Tuesday wave, 21-day advance, 7-night stay, logged-out cash menu), one booking channel, twelve monthly instants — and the flagship United gap statistic is a single December-wave passenger-weighted median of within-flight spreads (prereg §11(A)), with the 11 other waves demoted to 'descriptive dispersion' and excluded from the estimate. The published object, however, is a claim about what it costs bottom-40% households to replicate last year's experience — an inference from the grid point to a population quantity across routes, dates, advance windows, and channels the grid never touches. Refusing standard errors does not remove that inference; it leaves it unquantified. The grocery null is 12 items, 2 retailers, 1 ZIP; the streaming panel is 3 sellers; the banking control is 5 banks. A hostile statistician will say: every one of these is a purposive convenience sample wearing census language, and the pre-registration's own honesty about the paired-quote contrast ('family-specific pricing shocks do not cancel... never dressed in census language it hasn't earned,' prereg §4) shows the team knows the distinction and applies it selectively.

**Suggested response posture.** Concede the framing error and re-scope the census claim explicitly to 'exhaustive at the pre-registered coordinates.' Add a design-based robustness section (perturb wave day, advance window, route subset; publish the spread of the gap statistic across the 11 non-December waves as an honest volatility band around the December reading). Do not invent standard errors — publish coordinate-sensitivity instead, and say in every release which population statement the panel does and does not license.

#### [statistical-inference] The gates are low-severity tests: bands wide enough to be nearly unfailable, and a sensitivity grid truncated exactly where failures begin

**Objection.** G0's pass is marketed as the strongest exhibit, but a severity analysis guts it. The sector-gap band [+1.0, +6.0]pp spans a factor of six; the profile band [+4%, +14%] a factor of 3.5; and the band was constructed FROM the external benchmarks (B1/B2) that also generated the inputs — the phase0 note concedes the benchmarks were 'deliberately in the band file.' Given any inputs in the publicly documented range, the ex-ante probability of passing was high, so 'all four tests hold' is weak corroboration: the test had little chance of failing even if the method were mismeasuring. Worse, the retro sensitivity grid (data/retro-g0-result-2026-07-04.md) stops at 55% incidence — precisely the boundary where failures start (55% x $250 = +6.60pp, the sole out-of-band corner) — and 55% is exactly the only survey-based check-rate in the incidence register ('~55% check some or all,' 2023, labeled sanity-only). For a 2008 pre-fee base period, incidence above 45% is a priori plausible (checking was free; that is the era's whole point), yet 60–70% is never explored. The same pattern recurs in G1: GB-archival's ±0.5pp band sits over a quantity GAO puts at ~0.01pp/yr; GB-i's [−0.5, +2.0]pp is an order of magnitude wider than the expected wedge; GB-ii's 3.5pp tolerance is calibrated against dispersion the document itself says is routine. Wide pre-committed bands are honest about not tuning, but they buy almost no evidential power, and the packet nowhere distinguishes 'pre-committed' from 'severe.'

**Suggested response posture.** Adopt in part, defend in part. Concede that pass probability was high by construction and publish an ex-ante severity note for every existing and future band (what parameter configurations would have failed, and what prior mass they carry). Extend the incidence grid past the failure boundary and show it. Defend the design choice — wide bands were chosen to catch gross method failure, not to certify precision — but downgrade the public language from 'the method is validated' to 'the method is not grossly wrong,' which is all a wide band can certify.

#### [statistical-inference] The min operator harvests noise: the cross-seller series and single-session ULCC observations bias the coping margin and multiply silent comparisons

**Objection.** S is defined as the cross-carrier minimum over single-session carrier-direct captures per route (spec §2.2; prereg §1 channel harmonization), and H − S is published as the brand-switching coping margin (spec §6 row 6). The minimum of noisy draws is an extreme statistic: with per-carrier session-level price noise (A/B tests, dynamic repricing, cache states), E[min] falls below the minimum of the true posted prices, and the bias grows with the number of carriers per route — so H − S mechanically overstates the coping margin, more on competitive routes than hub routes, contaminating exactly the cross-route comparison the hub-span stratification invites. The design half-knows this: December ULCC ancillaries get a median-of-three-sessions rule (prereg §4) precisely because single sessions are unreliable — but the other eleven months' ULCC records, and every fare quote feeding S in every month, remain single-session draws declared to BE the menu. Separately, event detection runs an automated diff over ~45 cells x a full attribute dictionary x 12 waves (prereg §6 step 5) — hundreds of implicit comparisons a year. The anti-promotion confirmation rule disciplines schedule-posted fees, but dynamic in-flow prices are exempted by rule ('no multi-wave screen applies'), and there is no family-wise discipline on which detected diffs graduate into published exhibit rows, nor any stated false-discovery expectation for T3's 'attributable to the menu change' judgment.

**Suggested response posture.** Adopt. Extend the median-of-multiple-sessions rule to every observation that feeds S or any published statistic (or publish S as an interval acknowledging order-statistic bias with the per-route carrier count disclosed). Pre-register the exhibit-selection rule for detected events (e.g., all events above a size floor publish, none below — no discretion), and state the expected count of spurious single-wave diffs per year so readers can benchmark the ledger's volume.

#### [official-statistics-institutional] The comparator is a moving target the project neither controls nor has break rules for — and the measure's success accelerates its own obsolescence

**Objection.** Every published object is defined as a spread over specific CPI item-stratum series under specific BLS quote specifications, and the weights chain is pinned to R-CPI-I — a BLS research series with no continuity mandate whose 2026 update the packet already notes as delayed (spec §13 item 5). The codebook's comparator-continuity convention (§6) handles exactly one contingency: the never-published October 2025 index. It has no rule for the contingencies an NSO actually generates: a quote-specification change (BLS adding carry-on or second-bag to the airline spec — which would close Seam 1 and zero the flagship without ceremony), a stratum redefinition, a methodology migration to transaction data, or the quiet discontinuation of R-CPI-I, which would orphan the pinned weight recipe and the population definition itself. Note the perverse incentive structure: the project's most visible success — publicly demonstrating a seam — is precisely what makes BLS's cheapest institutional response a spec fix, and the packet nowhere states whether comparator improvement is scored as the project's success (obsolescence by design) or as a series break, nor how the published Gap would record it. An actual BLS reply would add the rest of the standard playbook: the concept defense (CPI approximates a COLI per CNSTAT At What Price; a conditional replication-cost index deliberately rejects the substitution and quality-adjustment machinery that framework requires, so a 'Gap' brand insinuates deficiency where there is concept difference — a point spec §1 concedes in text and the branding un-concedes); the genre precedent (Greenlees's 'Addressing Misconceptions About the CPI' exists because this genre recurs, and one triangulated-quote slip licenses dismissing the whole apparatus in a single MLR note); and the engagement trap — the GA rule excludes current BLS employees, so BLS can truthfully say it was never consulted about characterizations of its own methods, and the project has no plan for the day BLS's press office is asked to respond.

**Suggested response posture.** Adopt the mechanics, defend the concept. Pre-register a comparator-regime-change protocol in the codebook now (detection, dating as a structural break, dual-publication across the break, and an explicit celebratory-obsolescence clause: a seam closed by BLS is recorded as the measure's success); add an R-CPI-I discontinuation fallback for the weight recipe (e.g., own-computed PUMD replication, already Phase 2's plan); send BLS a courtesy pre-publication notice of every methodology characterization with an invitation to correct — not for approval, but so 'they never contacted us' is off the table; and keep the no-current-BLS-employees review rule while adding a standing correction channel BLS could use without endorsing anything.

#### [funding/independence] Reviewers are asked to sign off while the payer is undisclosed — including the payer of their own honoraria

**Objection.** The plan states plainly that 'dollars and payer are unresolved — WS-H's August budget memo is the forcing function,' and the memo offers discussants a 'paid formal review (honorarium per the project budget memo)' in the same August window. So the sequencing asks three named economists to lend their reputations to a distribution-charged price measure — and to accept payment — before anyone, including them, knows who is paying for the project, the counsel hours, the scraping infrastructure, or their own checks. Whatever the eventual answer, the gap itself is the vulnerability: if funding lands from an advocacy-adjacent, labor-aligned, or industry-aligned source after sign-offs are collected, every pre-commitment in the packet gets re-read as theater and the reviewers get retroactively compromised — the exact mechanism by which think-tank measurement work gets discounted regardless of quality. The packet's disclosure discipline is comprehensive about data provenance and silent about money provenance; there is no funding-disclosure commitment, no conflict policy, no statement of what funding sources the project would refuse. For a measure whose entire thesis is that undisclosed line items distort the picture, the irony will not be missed.

**Suggested response posture.** Adopt before outreach, not after. Resolve or bound the payer question in the August budget memo and disclose it in the outreach letter itself; publish a standing funding-and-conflicts policy (sources taken, sources refused, disclosure cadence) with the same versioned formality as the codebook; and offer reviewers the option of unpaid review with the honorarium disclosed either way, so no sign-off can later be characterized as purchased.
