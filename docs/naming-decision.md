# Naming decision record

`2026-07-04 · workstream I · decision by owner, recorded for the open-build trail`

## Decision

- **Public brand: "The Restoration Gap"** (kept). Formal measure name: Restoration
  Index / RX. Repo codename `bpi` retained (predates the naming work).
- **Tagline (travels with the name): _"what it costs to keep last year's life."_**
  Applied to the site brand lockup (all pages), the About page ("Why
  'restoration'?"), and the README.

## Why keep the name

An earlier collision scan found "Restoration Gap" **clear** in the economics/
consumer space (only benign cross-field use in dentistry/ecology), distinctive,
and credible in register — while the prior "Better Price Index / BPI" was
**blocked** (Bank Policy Institute, Bullish Percent Index, ESPN BPI, British
Phonographic Industry; "Better ___ Index" reads as the OECD family, and "Better"
is a superiority claim the methodology disclaims).

## The concern, and the fix

The name's one real weakness: "restoration" is precise but not self-evident — a
stranger may hear furniture refinishing or ecology. Rather than trade a clean,
distinctive, credible name for a more literal but weaker one, the fix is a
tagline that carries the meaning wherever the name appears. This keeps the
scanned-clean name and the domain (restorationgap.org) while removing the
comprehension gap.

## Alternatives considered (not chosen)

Ten were tabled. The self-evident contenders — **Same-Life Index** (most
intuitive, but soft and brushes the OECD "Better Life" family), **Effective-Price
Gap** (precise but jargony; EPI acronym crowded), **Fine Print Index** (memorable
but reads fees-only) — each traded away something the incumbent already has.
The advocacy-leaning options (Hidden Hike, Keeping-Up) were rejected as
undercutting the rigor-first credibility strategy.

## Follow-up

The unchosen alternatives were not collision- or domain-scanned; if the name is
ever revisited, scan finalists for confirmed availability before committing.
